Tweet 2 (131 chars)

Before leave, I ran operations like a lot of heads of ops do.

In every thread.

On every decision.

Online until late most nights.

Tweet 3 (136 chars)

Then I was gone for 6 months.

I worried the team would stall.

They didn't.

They made calls without me.

Most of them were good calls.

Tweet 4 (147 chars)

1. I delegate more.

Before, I kept work because doing it myself felt faster.

Now I hand it off early.

The team already proved they can carry it.

Tweet 5 (154 chars)

2. I decide faster.

I used to wait for one more data point.

Now I have less time and more clarity.

A good decision today beats a perfect one next week.

Tweet 6 (137 chars)

3. I stopped answering Slack after 6pm.

No exceptions for "quick questions."

Nothing has broken.

Most 9pm messages can wait until 9am.

Tweet 7 (143 chars)

The lesson:

Leave didn't make me less effective.

It showed me which parts of my job only I can do.

The list was much shorter than I thought.

Tweet 8 (122 chars)

You don't need 6 months away to learn this.

Pick 1 thing you'd hand off if you vanished tomorrow.

Hand it off this week.

Tweet 9 (196 chars)

TL;DR:

1. Delegate before you're forced to
2. Decide with less data
3. Set a hard Slack cutoff
4. Your team can carry more than you think
