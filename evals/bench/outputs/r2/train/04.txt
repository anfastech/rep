How to negotiate with a vendor when you have almost no leverage:

4 moves I use as head of ops.

No big budget required.

Tweet 2 (173 chars)

Small companies hear "take it or leave it" a lot.

We're 60 people.

Most of our vendors are bigger than us.

But leverage isn't only size.

It's timing, terms, and options.

Tweet 3 (177 chars)

Step 1: Anchor on their renewal deadline.

Vendors have quotas and quarter-ends.

Your renewal date matters more to them than you think.

Start talking 90 days before it, not 9.

Tweet 4 (165 chars)

Step 2: Bundle multi-year terms.

A vendor values a 3-year commitment far more than 1 year.

Offer the commitment.

In return, ask for a price lock and better terms.

Tweet 5 (163 chars)

Step 3: Bring a credible alternative.

Not a bluff.

A real quote from a real competitor.

If you won't switch, they'll know.

If you could switch, they'll listen.

Tweet 6 (190 chars)

Step 4: Ask for service credits, not discounts.

Discounts lower the contract value.

Credits often don't.

So credits are easier for your rep to approve.

You get the value without a fight.

Tweet 7 (197 chars)

Common mistakes to avoid:

→ Starting 2 weeks before renewal
→ Bluffing about a competitor you never called
→ Asking for "a better price" with no structure
→ Negotiating without finance in the loop

Tweet 8 (192 chars)

What to expect:

You won't win every point.

But you'll stop accepting the first quote.

And you'll trade what they value for what you need.

That's what leverage looks like when you're small.
