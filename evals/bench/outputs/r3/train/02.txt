We pulled 18 months of support tickets and tagged each one by where the customer was in the product when they got stuck. I expected the tickets to be spread fairly evenly.

They weren't. 41% of all tickets came from the same 3 onboarding screens, the ones a new customer sees in their first hour with us.

When we read those tickets side by side, they were mostly the same few questions asked in slightly different words. For a year and a half we had treated that volume as something support simply had to absorb. Nobody had thought of it as a problem with those three screens, because each ticket on its own looked like a one-off.

So we rewrote the screens. Total ticket volume dropped by about a third.

The analysis took a few weeks. The rewrite took less time than that. The hard part was looking at the tickets by location instead of by date.

If your support queue feels permanently full, try tagging a month of tickets by screen before you hire anyone else to answer them.
