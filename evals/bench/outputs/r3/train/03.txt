For one month I cancelled every recurring meeting on my calendar. There were 22 of them. The rule was simple: if something needed a meeting, someone had to book it fresh.

Four came back.

The four that came back had something in common. Each time, people showed up because they needed a decision made, and the meeting was the fastest way to get it. Most of the other 18 turned into a Slack thread. The rest just quietly stopped mattering, and nobody asked for them back.

I don't think recurring meetings are a bad idea. They're very easy to create, though, and ending one is nobody's job. So they pile up, and a calendar slowly fills with meetings that made sense once.

I'm not suggesting everyone cancel everything. A month was long enough for me to find out which meetings people actually missed, and short enough that nothing broke while we waited.

The short version: 22 meetings, 4 survivors. Most of my calendar was habit.
