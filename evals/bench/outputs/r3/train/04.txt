We're a 60-person company. When our vendor contracts come up for renewal, we are rarely anyone's biggest account, and we rarely hold much bargaining power.

Here's what has worked for us anyway:

1. Work around their renewal deadline, not yours. Start the conversation well before it. Their rep has a quarter to close, and the closer that date gets, the more a signed renewal matters to them.

2. Offer multi-year terms as a bundle. A two- or three-year commitment is worth a lot to a vendor. Don't hand it over for free. Trade it for better terms on everything else.

3. Bring a credible alternative. That means an actual quote or a demo you actually sat through. Vendors can tell when you're bluffing, and a bluff that gets called leaves you in a worse spot than before.

4. Ask for service credits instead of discounts. A discount tends to disappear at the next renewal. Credits tied to uptime or response times are often easier for the rep to approve, and they give you something to point to when service slips.

None of this makes you a bigger customer. It does make you a harder one to say no to.
