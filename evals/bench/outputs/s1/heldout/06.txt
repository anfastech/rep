6 months ago I left for parental leave. Since coming back, I haven't answered a single Slack message after 6pm.

Before the leave I was online most evenings. Running operations at a logistics software company, I believed something was always about to break and I had to be there when it did.

Then I was gone for half a year, and the company kept running.

That fact changed more about how I work than any productivity system I've tried. Three things are different now.

I delegate more. Work I used to keep because "it's faster if I do it" now goes to the people who covered it while I was out. They were doing it fine for 6 months.

I decide faster. With a hard stop at 6pm, I don't have the evening to keep turning a decision over, so I make it with what I have.

I stopped answering Slack after 6pm. The first couple of weeks that felt like neglect. Nothing broke.

The uncomfortable part is that I needed 6 months away to see it. My team didn't need me online at 9pm. I needed to feel needed, and I'd been calling that commitment.

I'm a better head of operations now than I was before I left, and I don't think that's a coincidence.

What did time away from work show you that you couldn't see while you were in it?
