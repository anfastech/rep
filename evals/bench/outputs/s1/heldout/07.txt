Roughly $200,000 in late-delivery penalties never hit our books last year, because a junior analyst on our team built a spreadsheet.

To that analyst: thank you. This one's for you.

The spreadsheet predicts which trucks will be late two days before it happens. It gets it right about 80% of the time.

It's a simple spreadsheet, and it now runs every morning before most of us have opened our laptops.

Before it existed, we usually found out a truck was late on the day it was late. At that point the penalty was already coming and all we could do was apologize.

Two days of warning changes the options. There's time to reroute, to call the customer and reset the date, to move the load to a carrier who can make it.

80% can sound modest. On a delivery that's about to miss its window, being right 4 times out of 5 is worth a lot of money.

What I keep thinking about is who built it. A junior person saw a problem we'd been paying for and built something small that fixed most of it.

Some of the best operations tools I've seen started as someone's spreadsheet. This one is now part of how we run every day.

Who on your team built something small that ended up running the place?
