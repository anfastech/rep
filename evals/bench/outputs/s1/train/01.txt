Two of our 12 warehouses couldn't ship an order for a full day, and the call that caused it was mine.

We had a new scanning app. It passed every test we ran before launch, so I signed off on rolling it out to all 12 sites on the same morning.

Staggering it felt slow. I wanted the whole network on one system and I didn't want to run two processes side by side for weeks.

By mid-morning two site managers were on the phone with me at once. Scanners weren't syncing and the trucks at both docks had nothing to load.

We got both sites back the next day. By then 3,000 orders had shipped late.

Every bug we hit that day would have shown up at a single site in a single shift. The other 11 sites could have covered the volume while we fixed it.

So the rule on my team now: nothing goes to 12 sites until it has survived one. Real orders, a real shift, the people who will actually use it.

It adds a few weeks to every rollout. I'll pay that every time after seeing what speed cost us.
