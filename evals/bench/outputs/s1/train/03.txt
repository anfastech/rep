22 recurring meetings were on our calendars. I cancelled every one of them for a month.

The rule was simple. If something needed a meeting, someone had to book it from scratch and write one line on why.

I expected the calendar to fill right back up by the second week. A few people on the team told me we'd lose track of half our projects.

After 30 days, 4 of the 22 came back.

The other 18 never got rebooked.

I think most companies hold too many recurring meetings, and the reason is structural. A recurring invite has to justify itself once, on the day it's created. After that it just keeps happening, long after the reason for it is gone.

The 4 that came back earned their place. Somebody missed each of them enough to send a new invite and explain why.

Cancelling them also meant facing how many hours I'd spent running and sitting in calls that, 18 times out of 22, didn't need to exist.

Which of your recurring meetings would survive a month of not existing?
