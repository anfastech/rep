Most ops leads walk into a vendor renewal with almost nothing to bargain with and reach for one of two moves.

Accepting the price teaches the vendor you'll sign whatever they send next year. Threatening to walk only works if they believe you, and they usually know your switching cost better than you do.

Both fail for the same reason: they turn the negotiation into a fight over one number.

I've been on the weak side of that table more times than I'd like. So I run a different play, in four moves:

1. Anchor on the vendor's renewal deadline. Their sales team has a quarter to close and a number to hit. Start the conversation early enough that their deadline arrives before yours does.

2. Bundle multi-year terms. A 2 or 3 year commitment is worth a lot to them, so make them pay for it in price.

3. Bring a credible alternative. A real quote from a real competitor you'd actually move to. They can tell when you're bluffing.

4. Ask for service credits, not discounts. A discount gets clawed back at the next renewal. A service credit ties their money to how well they perform.

None of these need bargaining power you don't have. They just move the conversation off the one number the vendor controls.
