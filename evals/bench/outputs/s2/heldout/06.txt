I came back to work after 6 months of parental leave.

The company had not changed much.

I had.

Before I left, I thought being a good head of operations meant being in everything. Every thread. Every decision. Every late message.

Six months away proved how much of that was optional.

The team ran without me. Decisions got made. Problems got solved. Some of them better than I would have solved them.

So three things are different now.

I delegate more.
Not because I have to, but because I saw what happens when I get out of the way. People step up when there is room to step into.

I decide faster.
I have less time, so I stopped circling. Most decisions do not need another week of thinking. They need someone to make a call and move.

I stopped answering Slack after 6pm.
That one felt hard for about a week. Then nothing broke. The messages were still there in the morning, and most of them had sorted themselves out.

I used to think these were trade-offs. Less control, less thinking time, less availability.

They are not trade-offs. They made me better at the job.

I did not need six months away to learn this. But it took six months away for me to see it.
