The most valuable tool we built last year was a spreadsheet.

A junior analyst on my team made it.

No new platform. No big project. No budget request.

Just a simple spreadsheet that looks at our data and predicts which trucks will be delayed, two days before it happens.

It is right about 80% of the time.

That sounds modest until you see what two days buys you in logistics. Two days is enough to reroute, rebook, warn the customer, and avoid the penalty.

So we started using it.

Now it runs every morning. The ops team checks it before anything else.

Last year it saved us roughly $200,000 in penalties.

I keep coming back to how this happened.

Nobody asked for it. Nobody wrote a spec. A junior analyst saw a problem, had the data, and built the simplest thing that could work.

If we had treated this as a proper project, it would still be in planning.

Three things I took from it:

The best ideas often come from the people closest to the data.
Simple and used every day beats clever and still in progress.
Juniors build great things when someone takes their work seriously.

My job was not to build it. My job was to notice it, back it, and get it into the morning routine.

Look at what your most junior people are building on the side. One of them might already have your next $200,000 idea.
