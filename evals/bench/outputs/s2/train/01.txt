I rolled out a new warehouse scanning app to all 12 of our sites on the same day.

Two of them went down for a full day.

3,000 orders shipped late.

The app was fine. The plan was the problem.

I had tested it. I had trained people. I had a rollback written down. What I did not have was a single site that had lived with it first.

So every surprise hit everywhere at once. Every site lead was firefighting at the same time, and nobody had a working example to copy.

Here is what a one-site pilot would have bought us:

A real shift, with real volume, on real hardware.
The small problems found while they were still small.
A site lead who could tell the other 11 exactly what to expect.
A rollback that touched one building, not twelve.

All of that for the cost of a few weeks.

Instead we paid with a day of downtime at two sites and 3,000 customers who got their orders late.

Speed felt like the responsible choice. Everyone wanted the new app. Waiting felt like dragging my feet.

It was not. Going everywhere at once only looks fast until something breaks.

Now the rule on my team is simple. Nothing new goes to every site until it has survived one.

Pilot at one site first. Then roll out.
