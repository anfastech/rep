Most recurring meetings would not survive being cancelled.

I know because I cancelled all of mine.

Every recurring meeting on my calendar, gone for a month. 22 of them.

The rule was simple. If a meeting mattered, someone would ask for it back.

Only 4 came back.

18 meetings that sat on calendars every week, with agendas and invites and people blocking time for them, and nobody missed them enough to ask.

Here is what I think happens.

A recurring meeting is easy to create and awkward to end. It starts for a good reason. A project, a launch, a problem that needed eyes on it every week. Then the reason fades, and the meeting stays.

Nobody wants to be the person who cancels it. So it rolls on, one week at a time.

The cost is not just the hour. It is the hour multiplied by everyone in the room, plus the prep, plus the context switching on either side.

The 4 that came back are better now. People asked for them on purpose, so they arrive knowing why they are there.

The other 18 turned into a message, a shared doc, or nothing at all. Work did not slow down.

My take: a recurring meeting should have to earn its place again, not keep it by default.

Cancel them all for a month. Let the ones that matter come back.
