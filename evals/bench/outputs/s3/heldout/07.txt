A junior analyst on our team saved us roughly $200,000 last year with a spreadsheet.

Late trucks are expensive for us. When a delivery misses its window, we pay a penalty, and by the time a delay is obvious, there's usually nothing left to do except pay it.

The spreadsheet predicts which trucks will be late two days before it happens. It's right about 80% of the time. Two days is enough warning to reroute, rebook, or tell the customer early, and those penalties are what added up to the $200,000.

It now runs every morning.

It's wrong about one time in five, and we're fine with that. Acting early on a false alarm costs us a phone call. Missing a real delay costs us a penalty.

I keep thinking about how simple it is. If we had scoped this as a formal project, it would have needed a budget and a quarter of planning before anyone looked at the data. One analyst looked at the data first and built something we could test the next morning.

If someone junior on your team is building a tool on the side, ask to see it. Ours is now part of how we start every day.
