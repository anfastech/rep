Unpopular opinion: most companies hold too many recurring meetings, and nobody notices because each one looks reasonable on its own.

I tested this on myself. I cancelled every recurring meeting for a month and told people to bring back only the ones they missed. When the month ended, 4 of the 22 came back.

Three reasons I think this happens everywhere:

A recurring meeting outlives the problem it was created for. Someone sets up a weekly sync during a launch or a hard quarter, the pressure passes, and the invite keeps renewing.

Nobody owns the decision to end it. Declining feels rude, and cancelling a meeting someone else created feels worse, so the default is to keep showing up.

The cost is spread thin. Thirty minutes a week looks small on one calendar. Multiply it by everyone in the room and by 22 invites, and it is a real share of the team's week.

What I'd recommend instead is a reset. Cancel every recurring meeting for a fixed period, say a month, and let people rebuild only the ones they ask for. The 4 that came back for us earned their place because someone wanted them back. The other 18 haven't been missed.
