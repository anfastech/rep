How to run a vendor negotiation when you have little leverage:

As head of operations at a 60-person company, I'm rarely the biggest customer on any vendor's list. These four moves still work for us.

1. Anchor on the vendor's renewal deadline. Their sales team is measured on closing renewals by a date. Start talking early enough that the deadline puts more pressure on them than on you.

2. Bundle multi-year terms. If you were going to stay anyway, a longer commitment costs you very little and is worth a lot to them. Trade it for price protection or better terms.

3. Bring a credible alternative. A competitor you've actually demoed, with a quote in hand, changes the conversation. A vague "we're looking around" usually doesn't, because vendors hear it every week.

4. Ask for service credits instead of a discount. A discount shows up in their revenue numbers, so the rep has to fight for it internally. Service credits are easier for them to approve, and they pay you back at the moment you need it most, when the service falls short.

Small companies tend to assume they have to take the first renewal quote. In most of our renewals, the deadline and a real alternative did more for us than our size ever could.
