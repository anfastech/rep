## LinkedIn Post

I came back from 6 months of parental leave a different kind of leader. 👶

Before leave, I was involved in everything. Every decision ran through me. Every Slack thread had my name in it. I answered messages late into the evening because I believed that's what a head of operations does.

Then I stepped away for 6 months.

The team kept running. Work got done. Decisions got made without me. That was humbling, and honestly, freeing.

Now that I'm back, three things have changed:

Key takeaways:
• I delegate more. The team proved they can own things, so I hand them off fully and stop checking in every hour.
• I decide faster. Most decisions are reversible. Moving quickly beats waiting for perfect information.
• I stopped answering Slack after 6pm. Evenings belong to my family, and the 9pm message almost always works at 9am.

None of these changes were planned. They came from coming back with less time and a clearer sense of what matters. When your evenings belong to a small person, you stop spending energy on work that doesn't need you.

What surprised me most is that none of this made me less effective. I'm more focused in the hours I work, and my team has more room to grow.

Many leaders hold on too tightly because they never get a reason to let go. Leave gave me one. You don't have to wait for yours.

If you stepped away from work for 6 months, which habit would you come back without?
