**Tweet 3/10**
What actually happened:

Two of our sites had setups nobody had mapped. Different network configs. Different scanner hardware. Different habits on the floor.

The app worked everywhere we'd checked. It broke where we hadn't.

**Tweet 4/10**
By the time we knew, it was too late to roll back cleanly.

Both sites were dark for a full day. Pickers were standing around. Trucks were waiting.

Result: 3,000 orders out the door late.

**Tweet 5/10**
The painful part wasn't the bug.

Bugs happen. The pain was the blast radius.

With 12 sites live at once, every problem hits everyone, and your team can't fix two fires while watching ten more.

**Tweet 6/10**
Lesson 1: Pilot at one site first.

Pick one real site. Run the new tool there for a set period. Let the floor team break it before 11 other sites depend on it.

**Tweet 7/10**
Lesson 2: Your test environment is not your warehouse.

The edge cases live on the floor: the old scanner in aisle 9, the Wi-Fi dead zone, the shift that does things its own way.

A pilot finds those. A demo never will.

**Tweet 8/10**
Lesson 3: Have a rollback plan before go-live, not after.

If a site goes down, how fast can you get it back to the old system? Minutes or a full day?

That answer should decide your rollout speed.

**Tweet 9/10**
Lesson 4: Slow is fast.

One pilot site feels like a delay. But a staged rollout would have cost us a few weeks.

The big-bang launch cost us a full day at two sites and 3,000 late orders.

**Tweet 10/10**
Pilot at one site. Learn. Then scale.

It's the cheapest lesson I've paid full price for.

## LinkedIn Post

I took two warehouses offline for a full day. Here's what it taught me. 👇

We had a new warehouse scanning app. It was faster, cleaner, and our team was excited about it. Testing went well. So I made a call: roll it out to all 12 sites at once.

Why make 11 sites wait for something that works?

Here's why.

Two of our sites had setups we hadn't fully mapped. The app ran fine everywhere we had tested. It failed where we hadn't. Both sites were down for a full day, and 3,000 orders shipped late.

The bug wasn't the real problem. The blast radius was. With every site live at the same time, there was no safe place to learn and no quiet place to fix things.

What I do differently now:

Key takeaways:
• Pilot at one site first. Let a real floor team find the edge cases.
• Your test environment is not your warehouse. Hardware, network, and habits differ by site.
• Write the rollback plan before go-live. Know how fast you can return to the old system.
• Treat slow as fast. A staged rollout costs weeks. A failed big-bang launch costs customers.

Today, every new tool we launch starts at one site. We watch it closely, fix what breaks, and only then move to the next wave.

I still believe in moving quickly. But speed in operations means speed to a stable result, not speed to launch day.

What's a rollout lesson you learned the hard way? I'd like to hear it in the comments.
