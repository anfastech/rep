## LinkedIn Post

41% of our support tickets came from just 3 screens. 📊

For a long time, we treated rising support volume as a cost of growth. More customers, more questions. We assumed the answer was more people.

Then we looked at the data.

We went through 18 months of support tickets and tagged each one by where the customer got stuck. The pattern was clear: 41% of all tickets came from the same 3 onboarding screens.

Almost half of our support load was coming from the first pages every new customer sees.

So we didn't add headcount. We didn't write another help article. We rewrote those 3 screens with plainer language, fewer steps, and clearer instructions right where people got confused.

Ticket volume dropped by a third.

Key takeaways:
• Your support queue is product feedback. Read it in aggregate, not one ticket at a time.
• Confusion during onboarding is a first-impression problem, not just a support cost.
• Fix the source before you scale the response. Rewriting screens beat hiring.

Most teams already have this data. It's sitting in the helpdesk, waiting to be counted.

The work itself wasn't fancy. Tag the tickets, rank the sources, and fix the top few. But it changed how our support and product teams talk to each other. Tickets are now an input to the roadmap, not just a queue to clear.

When did your team last analyze support tickets as a dataset? What did you find?
