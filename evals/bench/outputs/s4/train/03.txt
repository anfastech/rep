## LinkedIn Post

I cancelled every recurring meeting for a month. Only 4 of 22 came back.

Here's my contrarian take: most companies hold far too many recurring meetings.

Not because anyone plans it. A project kicks off, so someone sets up a weekly sync. The project wraps, but the meeting stays. A few years later, calendars are packed with meetings nobody remembers creating.

So I tested it.

For one month, I cancelled every recurring meeting on my calendar. 22 in total. The only rule: if a meeting was truly needed, someone had to ask for it back.

I expected confusion. Instead, the work kept moving. Updates happened in writing. Blockers were raised in quick messages. When a live conversation was the fastest path, we just had one.

At the end of the month, only 4 of the 22 meetings came back.

Key takeaways:
• Recurring meetings outlive their purpose. Audit them on a schedule.
• Status updates rarely need a meeting. Decisions often do.
• The real cost is lost focus time, not just the minutes on the calendar.
• Make people opt back in. What's truly needed will return.

18 meetings were running on habit, not need. That's a lot of hours given back to the team, and a lot more uninterrupted time for the work that actually moves the business.

I plan to repeat this reset every year. Calendars fill up quietly, so they need to be cleared on purpose.

How many of your recurring meetings would survive a month without them? 🤔
