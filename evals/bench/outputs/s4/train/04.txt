## LinkedIn Post

What do you do when the vendor holds all the cards? 🃏

As head of operations at a 60-person company, I often sit across from vendors who are much bigger than us. We can't promise them huge spend. They know it, and we know it.

But I've learned that leverage isn't only about size. It also comes from timing, structure, and options.

Here's the playbook I use when we have little leverage:

1️⃣ Anchor on the vendor's renewal deadline. Their sales team has a quarter to close. Your renewal date puts pressure on them too. Start early and plan backward from that date.

2️⃣ Bundle multi-year terms. Vendors value predictable revenue. A longer commitment is a bargaining chip, so trade it for better terms. Don't give it away.

3️⃣ Bring a credible alternative. Not a bluff. A real option you've priced and could move to. Vendors can tell the difference.

4️⃣ Ask for service credits, not discounts. Discounts are hard for vendors to approve. Credits are often easier, and they hold the vendor accountable when service slips.

Key takeaways:
• Timing is leverage. Know their calendar, not just yours.
• Commitment is currency. Spend it on purpose.
• The best ask is the one the other side can say yes to.

Smaller doesn't mean powerless. It means you have to prepare better.

What's a tactic that helped you win against a bigger vendor?
