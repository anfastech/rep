# What 6 months away taught me about my job

*I delegate more, decide faster, and log off at 6. None of it was planned.*

I've been back at work for a few weeks now, after **six months of parental leave.**

Before I left, I wrote a handover document that was 14 pages long. I was sure something would break while I was gone. Mostly, nothing did. That was humbling.

This issue is a bit more personal than usual. It's about three things I do differently now, and why I don't plan to go back.

[Subscribe button]

## The team didn't need me the way I thought

My first week back, I asked for a list of everything that went wrong while I was out. The list was short. Each problem had been handled, and most were handled the way I would have.

A few were handled better.

It's a strange feeling. **You find out how much of your job was actually you, and how much was habit.** For me, a lot more was habit than I'd like to admit.

## 1. I delegate more

Before leave, I said I delegated. What I really did was hand things off and then quietly take them back when they got interesting.

While I was gone, nobody could hand anything back. So people just owned it. Site leads made calls I used to make. Our planning lead ran the quarterly review without me.

When I came back, I didn't take those things back. **If it ran fine for six months without me, it doesn't need me now.** That freed up about a third of my week.

## 2. I decide faster

I have less time now. Daycare pickup is not negotiable, and it doesn't care about my calendar.

So I stopped waiting for perfect information. Most decisions in ops can be undone. If a call is reversible, I make it with what I have and adjust later.

**Slow decisions felt careful. Mostly they were just slow.** The team says things move faster now, and I haven't seen anything go worse because of it.

## 3. I stopped answering Slack after 6pm

This was the hardest one.

Before leave, I answered messages late into the evening. I told myself it showed I was available. Really, it trained everyone to expect me at 9pm.

Now I log off at 6. If something is truly urgent, people call me. In the weeks since I started, nobody has called. **The late-night messages weren't urgent. They were just there.**

## What's still hard

I don't want to make this sound tidy. Some days I feel behind. Some days I miss the version of me who knew every detail.

But that version was a bottleneck. The team works better with me as a backstop than as a funnel.

* * *

The thing I'll keep from all of this: **time away shows you which parts of your job were really yours.** You don't need six months off to check. You just need to ask what would happen if you stopped.

Have you ever come back from a long break and worked differently? What stuck, and what slipped back? Hit reply. I'd love to hear how other people handled it.

If this was useful, forward it to a manager who's about to go on leave, or who just came back.

Maya
