# We opened an office in Lisbon

*Nine people are already there. Here's why we went, and what opening it taught me.*

This one is news, so I'll say it plainly: **we've opened our first office outside the US, in Lisbon.**

Nine people already work there. We're hiring 15 more this year, across engineering and operations.

If you've been reading this newsletter for a while, you know I don't do announcements often. This one gets an issue because opening the office was one of the biggest ops projects I've run, and I learned a lot doing it.

[Subscribe button]

## Why Lisbon

We looked at a handful of cities. Lisbon won for three plain reasons.

**The talent was there.** We met strong engineers and ops people who wanted to work on hard logistics problems, not just another consumer app.

**The time zone works.** Lisbon overlaps with our US team for a few hours every day. That's enough for a real handoff, not just an email thrown over the wall at midnight.

**People wanted to stay.** Several early candidates told us they'd moved there on purpose and planned to build a career there. That matters more than any cost spreadsheet.

## What opening an office actually involves

From the outside, a new office looks like a lease and a welcome lunch. From the inside, it's mostly paperwork and process.

A few things I'd tell anyone doing this for the first time:

**Set up the boring parts before the first hire.** Local entity, payroll, contracts, benefits, equipment. If any of these are late, your first nine people find out on day one.

**Write things down before you need them.** Our US team ran on a lot of hallway knowledge. That doesn't cross an ocean. We spent weeks turning "ask Dave" into actual documents, and the US team benefits from them too.

**Plan the handoff, not just the hours.** Overlap time is precious. We protect it for decisions and handoffs, and push status updates into writing.

## Who's there now

The first nine are a mix of engineers and operations people. Some came from logistics companies, some from software, a couple from neither.

**They aren't a satellite team.** They own real parts of the product and real parts of our operations. That was a rule from the start. A second office that only gets leftover work stops being worth having.

## We're hiring 15 more

Over the rest of this year, we're hiring 15 more people in Lisbon. **Engineering and operations roles**, at several levels.

If you want to work on software that moves real freight, and you'd rather your work show up on a loading dock than in a slide deck, take a look.

## Core voice characteristics
- Register: casual professional. Sounds like an ops lead talking in the hallway, not on a keynote stage.
- Avg sentence length: 11-14 words, with frequent short fragments.
- Humor: dry, mostly at her own expense.
- Confidence: declarative. Says what happened and what she does now. Hedges only on things she hasn't tested.

## Vocabulary
- Uses: sites, the floor, orders, rollout, the numbers, plain verbs (cut, ship, fix, kill, pull)
- Avoids: synergy, "leverage" as a verb, "excited to announce", game-changer, journey, exclamation marks, em dashes
- Technical level: assumes ops literacy; explains software terms in one clause

## Structural patterns
- Opens with: a specific moment or number, stated flat
- Closes with: one direct question to readers, signed "Maya"
- Paragraphs: 1-3 sentences
- Uses prose for stories; numbered lists only for steps

## Signature moves
- Puts the plain fact first, even in news: "This one is news, so I'll say it plainly."
